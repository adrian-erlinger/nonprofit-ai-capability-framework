# Decision Conversation v0.1

## Status

This is a Phase 0 facilitated beta protocol.

The current design is a working hypothesis. It will be revised based on observed beta-user behavior and feedback.

Version 0.1 should remain stable during the initial test cycle so results can be compared across sessions. Facilitators may adapt wording and follow relevant lines of inquiry, but should preserve the underlying reasoning domains and required outputs.

This document is not a validated assessment, fixed questionnaire, final product flow, or comprehensive consulting manual.

## Beta Purpose

The conversation helps one association leader move from one organizational concern to:

- A clarified outcome.
- A bounded workflow.
- A preliminary diagnosis.
- An AI-suitability conclusion.
- One responsible next move.
- A named owner.
- Evidence needed for the next decision.

The central question is: What organizational outcome are you trying to improve, and where can AI responsibly help, if at all?

## Scope

Version 0.1 is designed for:

- Member-based associations.
- Supportive and constituency-facing workflows.
- One team and one workflow per conversation.
- Approximately 30-45 minutes.
- Facilitator-led delivery.
- Low- to moderate-consequence workflows.

Representative starting situations include:

- Members and staff cannot find current information.
- Member communications are inconsistent or poorly targeted.
- New members do not understand or use their benefits.
- Staff repeatedly recreate existing content.
- Member feedback is difficult to synthesize.
- Sponsorship or proposal materials are scattered.
- Existing AI licenses produce little measurable value.
- Staff use AI without shared practices.

Version 0.1 excludes:

- Eligibility decisions.
- Employment decisions.
- Health or safety screening.
- Legal advice.
- High-consequence profiling.
- Fully autonomous member communications.
- Organization-wide maturity assessments.

## Opening Statement

Facilitator language:

"We will examine one organizational concern, identify the workflow behind it, and determine the most responsible next move. That may involve AI, prerequisite work, a non-AI intervention, or a decision not to proceed."

## Conversation Architecture

The eleven decision domains remain the reasoning model, but the user experiences them through six conversational phases.

The eleven domains are:

1. Starting situation.
2. Desired change.
3. Ownership and stakeholders.
4. Current workflow.
5. Root problem.
6. Candidate interventions.
7. AI suitability.
8. Prioritization.
9. Adoption design.
10. Bounded implementation cycle.
11. Institutionalization.

The six phases are designed for facilitated conversation, not rigid scripting. Facilitators should avoid translating the opening concern into an AI use case prematurely. They should separate observable symptoms from root-cause hypotheses, consider non-AI interventions before AI, and avoid creating a numerical score.

### 1. Frame The Concern

Decision domains covered:

- Starting situation.
- Desired change.
- Ownership and stakeholders.

Approximate time: 5-7 minutes.

Facilitator questions:

- What concern made this conversation feel worth having now?
- What organizational outcome are you trying to improve?
- Who experiences the problem most directly?
- Who has authority to change the work?
- What would be noticeably better if this changed?

Information to capture:

- Starting concern.
- Desired outcome.
- Source of urgency.
- Initial owner.
- Affected stakeholders.
- Any existing AI, technology, or governance context.

Facilitator guidance:

- Start in the participant's language.
- Do not assume the concern is an AI use case.
- Clarify whether the objective is organizational, departmental, programmatic, or workflow-specific.
- If the concern is broad, narrow toward one concrete area of work without forcing a solution.

### 2. Bound The Workflow

Decision domains covered:

- Current workflow.
- Ownership and stakeholders.

Approximate time: 5-8 minutes.

Facilitator questions:

- Where does this work start and end?
- What triggers the workflow?
- Which roles participate?
- What inputs, systems, or content does the workflow depend on?
- What output is produced, and for whom?
- Where does human judgment, approval, or escalation occur?
- What existing software, automation, or AI tools already touch this workflow?

Information to capture:

- Workflow boundary.
- Trigger.
- Roles.
- Inputs and systems.
- Output.
- Recipient or audience.

Use this workflow boundary sentence:

"When [trigger] occurs, [roles] use [inputs and systems] to produce [output] for [recipient]."

Facilitator guidance:

- Keep the boundary small enough to discuss in 30-45 minutes.
- Do not let the conversation expand into an organization-wide maturity assessment.
- If multiple workflows appear, name them and choose one for this session.

### 3. Diagnose The Problem

Decision domains covered:

- Current workflow.
- Root problem.

Approximate time: 6-8 minutes.

Facilitator questions:

- What observable symptoms tell you this workflow is not working well?
- Where do delays, rework, confusion, or quality problems appear?
- What do staff, members, or other stakeholders experience?
- What might be causing the symptoms?
- What evidence do you already have, and what is still assumed?
- How often does this happen, and at what volume?
- What do staff currently do to compensate or work around the problem?

Information to capture:

- Observable symptoms.
- Plausible root-cause hypotheses.
- Evidence already available.
- Evidence still needed.
- Consequences if the problem persists.
- Frequency or volume.
- Current workarounds.

Facilitator guidance:

- Separate observable symptoms from root-cause hypotheses.
- Mark assumptions as assumptions.
- Listen for content, process, ownership, training, governance, and technology issues.
- Avoid jumping from a symptom to an AI solution.

### 4. Compare Interventions And Assess AI Fit

Decision domains covered:

- Candidate interventions.
- AI suitability.

Approximate time: 7-10 minutes.

Facilitator questions:

- What non-AI changes might address the root problem?
- What existing tools or processes could be improved before adding AI?
- Where, specifically, might AI help?
- What would AI need to access, generate, summarize, classify, recommend, or route?
- What could go wrong if AI were used here?
- What human review or escalation would be needed?

Information to capture:

- Non-AI interventions considered.
- Possible AI-supported intervention.
- Material risks and constraints.
- Conditions that would make AI more or less suitable.
- Required human review.

Facilitator guidance:

- Consider non-AI interventions before AI.
- Apply risk, privacy, source-authority, accessibility, trust, human-review, staff-capacity, existing-technology, and maintenance checks only where material.
- Keep the analysis proportionate to the consequence and risk of the workflow.
- Do not create a numerical score.

### 5. Prioritize The Responsible Next Move

Decision domains covered:

- Prioritization.
- Adoption design.
- AI suitability.

Approximate time: 5-7 minutes.

Facilitator questions:

- Which option seems most responsible to pursue next?
- What would create the most useful learning with reasonable effort and risk?
- What must be true before AI should be tested here?
- Who would need to own the next move?
- What would make this next move worth doing?

Information to capture:

- AI-suitability conclusion.
- Responsible next move.
- Accountable owner.
- Prerequisites.
- Material adoption needs.

Facilitator guidance:

- Distinguish the AI-suitability conclusion from the responsible next move.
- Prioritization may consider impact, speed to value, effort, risk, readiness, strategic importance, learning value, and reusability.
- Keep the recommendation bounded and actionable.
- A responsible next move may be preparation, a non-AI intervention, deferral, or rejection of the proposed intervention.

### 6. Define The Bounded Cycle And Learning Record

Decision domains covered:

- Bounded implementation cycle.
- Institutionalization.
- Adoption design.

Approximate time: 5-7 minutes.

Facilitator questions:

- What evidence should inform the next decision?
- What would count as useful progress?
- What should be reviewed, by whom, and when?
- How will decisions and learning be recorded?
- If the intervention saves time or capacity, how should that capacity be used?
- What exactly will be completed or tested?
- What is explicitly outside scope?
- At the review point, what decision will be made: continue, adapt, standardize, expand, stop, or return to prerequisite work?

Information to capture:

- Evidence and success measures.
- Review date.
- Decision owner.
- Learning record.
- Use of released capacity.
- Next decision point.
- Defined scope.
- Explicit exclusions.
- Decision options at the review point.

Facilitator guidance:

- Treat the bounded cycle as a learning structure, not a guarantee of adoption.
- Preserve institutional memory by recording the decision, assumptions, constraints, and evidence needed next.
- Keep the output short enough to be useful after the session.

## AI-Suitability Conclusions

Use exactly these four provisional conclusions:

1. Proceed with an AI-supported workflow.
2. Prepare first, then reconsider AI.
3. Pursue a non-AI intervention.
4. Do not pursue the proposed intervention.

The fourth conclusion concerns the proposed intervention, not necessarily the underlying organizational objective.

## Responsible Next Moves

Responsible next moves may include:

- Conduct further workflow discovery.
- Assign an accountable owner.
- Improve data or content.
- Redesign the process.
- Train staff.
- Establish governance.
- Test a non-AI intervention.
- Test a bounded AI-supported workflow.
- Defer action.
- Reject the proposed intervention.

The AI-suitability conclusion and responsible next move are related but distinct. For example, a conversation may conclude "prepare first, then reconsider AI" and recommend content cleanup, ownership clarification, or further workflow discovery as the next move.

## Decision Brief

The facilitated conversation should produce a one- to two-page decision brief with the following structure:

- Organizational concern.
- Desired outcome.
- Selected workflow.
- Owner and stakeholders.
- Current-state diagnosis.
- Interventions considered.
- AI-suitability conclusion and rationale.
- Material risks and constraints.
- Responsible next move.
- Prerequisites.
- Evidence and success measures.
- Use of released capacity, if applicable.
- Review date and decision.

## Anchor Scenario

Starting situation:

"Members and staff cannot reliably find current, authoritative information because it is scattered across webpages, PDFs, the member portal, shared drives, and individual employees."

This scenario is useful for testing the model because it is common in associations, concrete enough to bound as a workflow, and likely to reveal content ownership, source authority, search, portal configuration, staff capacity, governance, and AI-suitability issues. It also tests whether the conversation can resist jumping to a chatbot before clarifying the workflow and sources of authority.

Provisional example outcome:

- AI conclusion: Prepare first, then reconsider AI.
- Responsible next move: Inventory the top 25 repeated member questions, identify authoritative sources and content owners, and test whether existing search or portal configuration resolves the problem.
- Possible later test: A governed retrieval assistant covering those questions, with citations, staff review, and an escalation path.

This is an illustrative outcome, not a predetermined result for beta users.

## Beta Validation Questions

1. Did the participant begin with a real organizational concern?
2. Did the conversation identify one bounded workflow?
3. Did it distinguish symptoms from plausible root causes?
4. Were non-AI interventions seriously considered?
5. Was the AI conclusion understandable and defensible?
6. Did the participant receive one concrete next move?
7. Could the participant name an accountable owner?
8. Were relevant risks addressed without making the process unnecessarily burdensome?
9. Did the output feel worth 30-45 minutes?
10. Would the participant take the proposed next action?

## Beta Observation Record

Facilitators should record observations after each session:

- Beta session ID.
- Actual session duration.
- Organization type.
- Participant role.
- Participant's opening statement in their own words.
- Starting concern.
- Workflow selected.
- AI-suitability conclusion.
- Responsible next move.
- Points of confusion.
- Questions that produced useful insight.
- Questions that felt repetitive or irrelevant.
- Material issues the protocol missed.
- Participant's stated value.
- Likelihood of taking the next action.
- Proposed change to test later.

Observations should be recorded after each session, but the core Version 0.1 protocol should not be rewritten between every interview. Proposed revisions should be accumulated and reviewed after the initial test cycle.

## Initial Test Cycle

The initial test cycle is approximately five to eight facilitated sessions with association leaders in different functions.

The purpose is to test:

- Comprehensibility.
- Usefulness.
- Adaptability across starting situations.
- Quality of the resulting next move.
- Facilitator burden.
- Whether the conversation provides enough value to support a consulting engagement or guided product.

Version 0.1 intentionally excludes recruitment design, pricing, software screens, commercial packaging, assessment scores, maturity stages, weighted rubrics, and automated decision rules.
