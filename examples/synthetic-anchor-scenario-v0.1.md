# Synthetic Anchor Scenario — Member Knowledge Access

## Synthetic Status

This is a fictional scenario created for internal practice and public illustration.

It does not describe a real organization, client, participant, or engagement. All names, metrics, systems, and circumstances are synthetic.

## Organization Profile

The National Association of Community Program Managers (NACPM) is a synthetic professional association with approximately 4,800 members and twelve full-time staff. Its members work in community programs, workforce development, education, and local service organizations.

NACPM offers professional development, member resources, webinars, research, a member directory, and an annual conference. It has a lean membership and communications team.

The synthetic association uses an association management system, a WordPress website, a member portal, Microsoft 365, SharePoint, shared drives, and email. Specific software vendors other than Microsoft 365 and WordPress are intentionally not named.

Three staff members have access to Microsoft 365 Copilot, but it has not been connected to an approved association knowledge base. Staff use public AI tools inconsistently for drafting, but there are no shared workflow or content-governance practices.

## Participant Profile

Jordan Lee is a fictional participant.

- Name: Jordan Lee
- Role: Director of Membership and Member Experience

## Opening Statement

"Members keep asking us questions that we know we have answered somewhere, but neither they nor our staff can reliably find the current answer."

## Why It Matters Now

Repeated questions consume staff capacity. Members receive inconsistent answers. Leadership is asking whether AI could improve member service.

NACPM already has limited Copilot access but no governed knowledge workflow. Staff are concerned that a chatbot could surface outdated or member-restricted information.

## Known Facts

All facts in this section are synthetic:

- Membership and program staff receive approximately 120 repeated information requests per month.
- Staff estimate that responding, searching, verifying, and routing these questions consumes approximately six staff hours per week.
- Answers are sometimes copied from outdated PDFs.
- Similar information exists in multiple formats and locations.
- Several important resources do not have a named content owner.
- The current website and portal search functions are inconsistent.
- Members sometimes receive different answers depending on which staff member responds.
- Some resources are public, while others are restricted to members.
- The workflow does not involve health, safety, eligibility, employment, or legal decisions.
- The association has not established an accuracy baseline.
- The organization does not yet know whether most questions could be resolved through existing search, improved content governance, traditional automation, or an AI-supported retrieval workflow.

## Unknowns and Assumptions

- Which questions account for most of the volume?
- Which sources are authoritative?
- How much content is outdated or duplicated?
- Whether current search configuration can be improved.
- Whether members prefer self-service or staff support for different questions.
- What accuracy threshold would be acceptable.
- What proportion of the six estimated hours could actually be recovered.
- Whether the participant has authority to assign content owners across departments.

## Practice Purpose

This scenario is designed to test whether Decision Conversation v0.1 can:

- Begin with an operational concern.
- Bound one workflow.
- Separate symptoms from root causes.
- Consider non-AI interventions first.
- Produce a proportionate AI-suitability conclusion.
- Define a concrete next move.
- Preserve uncertainty and evidence needs.
