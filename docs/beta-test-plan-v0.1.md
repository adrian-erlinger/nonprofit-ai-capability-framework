# Decision Conversation v0.1 — Beta Test Plan

## Status

This is the operating plan for the initial facilitated beta cycle.

The Decision Conversation protocol, facilitator worksheet, and decision brief are frozen as Version 0.1 during the cycle. Proposed changes will be recorded but reviewed only after the initial five to eight sessions.

The test is intended to generate evidence about the framework and user experience, not validate a finished product.

## Beta Objectives

The cycle will test:

- Whether participants can move from a real concern to one bounded workflow.
- Whether the conversation distinguishes symptoms from plausible root causes.
- Whether both AI-first and operational-problem starting points can be handled.
- Whether non-AI interventions receive serious consideration.
- Whether the AI-suitability conclusion is understandable and defensible.
- Whether the responsible next move is concrete and actionable.
- Whether the decision brief provides value.
- Whether the session can be completed in approximately 30-45 minutes.
- How much facilitator interpretation and effort are required.
- Whether the experience could support a consulting engagement or guided product.

## Participant Profile

Suitable participants are leaders or functional owners in member-based associations who:

- Own or materially influence a real workflow.
- Can discuss a current operational concern.
- Are willing to participate in a 45-minute facilitated session.
- Can provide candid feedback.
- Are working with a low- or moderate-consequence workflow.

Participants do not need AI expertise or a defined AI use case.

## Initial Participant Mix

The initial cycle should include approximately five to eight participants across:

- Membership or member experience.
- Marketing or communications.
- Operations or technology.
- Learning, programs, sponsorship, or business development.

Portfolio goals:

- At least one participant with an existing AI investment.
- At least one participant who does not begin with an AI request.
- At least one small or lean-staff association.
- At least one association with fragmented content, data, or systems.

This is a purposive beta sample, not a representative sector study.

## Suitable Starting Situations

Suitable starting situations may include:

- Members or staff cannot find current information.
- Member communications are inconsistent or poorly targeted.
- New members do not understand or use their benefits.
- Staff repeatedly recreate existing content.
- Member feedback is difficult to synthesize.
- Sponsorship or proposal materials are scattered.
- Existing AI licenses produce little measurable value.
- Staff use AI without shared practices.
- Manual workarounds conceal fragmented systems or broken workflows.

## Exclusions

This beta cycle excludes:

- Eligibility decisions.
- Employment decisions.
- Health or safety screening.
- Legal advice.
- High-consequence profiling.
- Fully autonomous member communications.
- Organization-wide maturity assessments.
- Participants seeking only a vendor recommendation or generic AI demonstration.

## Session Sequence

1. Confirm the participant has a real concern suitable for the beta scope.
2. Explain the purpose, working status, and confidentiality boundaries.
3. Run the Decision Conversation using the facilitator worksheet.
4. Complete the post-session observation record.
5. Produce the decision brief within 24 hours.
6. Share the brief with the participant.
7. Collect feedback on clarity, credibility, usefulness, and likelihood of action.
8. Store session materials privately.
9. Add anonymized observations to the accumulated beta evidence.
10. Do not change the core protocol between individual sessions.

## Participant Feedback Questions

1. Does the brief accurately represent the concern you brought into the conversation?
2. Did the conversation help clarify the actual problem?
3. Is the AI-suitability conclusion understandable?
4. Is the responsible next move credible and specific?
5. What part of the conversation was most useful?
6. What felt repetitive, unclear, or unnecessary?
7. What important issue was missed?
8. Would you take the proposed next action?
9. Would this experience be valuable enough to recommend to another association leader?
10. What additional support would you need after receiving the brief?

These questions may be asked conversationally rather than as a formal scored survey.

## Evidence to Capture

- Participant role and organization type.
- Participant's opening statement in their own words.
- Selected workflow.
- Actual session duration.
- AI-suitability conclusion.
- Responsible next move.
- Accountable owner identified or not identified.
- Points of confusion.
- Questions that produced insight.
- Questions skipped or judged irrelevant.
- Material issues missed.
- Participant's stated value.
- Likelihood of taking the next action.
- Facilitator burden.
- Time required to produce the decision brief.
- Follow-up support requested.

## Privacy and Repository Boundaries

The repository is public.

Participant names, contact information, organization-specific confidential details, completed worksheets, recordings, transcripts, and completed decision briefs must not be committed to the repository.

Actual session materials should be stored in a private DSL workspace outside the repository.

Public repository findings must be anonymized and aggregated. Synthetic examples may be used publicly when clearly labeled as synthetic. Any direct quotation used publicly requires participant permission.

Before the session, participants should be told that anonymized observations may be used to improve the framework and summarized in future research findings. Identifiable information and direct quotations will not be used publicly without permission. Sessions should not be recorded unless the participant gives explicit permission.

## Review Gate

The initial cycle should be reviewed after approximately five to eight sessions.

The review should determine:

- Which questions consistently produced value.
- Which questions were repetitive, unclear, or routinely skipped.
- Whether the six-phase structure worked.
- Whether the eleven-domain model captured material issues.
- Whether the four AI-suitability conclusions were sufficient.
- Whether the responsible-next-move categories were sufficient.
- Whether the decision brief was useful and appropriately concise.
- Whether the experience should remain facilitated, become AI-assisted, or support a guided self-service component.
- Whether a Version 0.2 revision is justified.

## Revision Standard

Changes should be based on repeated observations, material omissions, participant confusion, or failure to produce useful decisions.

Do not revise Version 0.1 solely because a facilitator prefers different wording in one session.

## Exit Criteria

The cycle is successful enough to proceed when:

- Most participants can identify a bounded workflow.
- Most sessions produce a defensible conclusion and responsible next move.
- Participants generally recognize their situation in the decision brief.
- The process reveals meaningful information beyond the opening concern.
- The facilitator can run the session without excessive explanation.
- The organization can identify what evidence or action comes next.

These are qualitative beta criteria, not numerical performance thresholds.

## Working Boundaries

This beta cycle does not test:

- Pricing.
- Sales conversion.
- Software screens.
- Automated decision logic.
- Organization-wide maturity scoring.
- Vendor selection.
- Full implementation services.

Recruitment materials, participant lists, pricing, consent documentation, application screens, and automated scoring are outside the scope of this beta test plan.
