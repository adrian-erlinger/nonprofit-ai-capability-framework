"We will examine one organizational concern, identify the workflow behind it, and determine the most responsible next move. That may involve AI, prerequisite work, a non-AI intervention, or a decision not to proceed."

- Participant's opening statement in their own words: "Members keep asking us questions that we know we have answered somewhere, but neither they nor our staff can reliably find the current answer."
- Why this matters now: Repeated questions are consuming staff time, members receive inconsistent answers, and leadership is asking whether AI could improve member service.

## Phase 3: Diagnose the Problem

Prompts:

- What observable symptoms show that the workflow is not working well?
- Where do delay, rework, confusion, risk, or quality problems appear?
- How often does this happen, and at what volume?
- What do staff or members experience?
- What workarounds are currently used?
- What might be causing the symptoms?
- What evidence exists, and what remains assumed?

### Observable Evidence

- Membership and program staff receive approximately 120 repeated information requests per month.
- Staff estimate that responding, searching, verifying, and routing these questions consumes approximately six staff hours per week.
- Answers are sometimes copied from outdated PDFs.
- Similar information exists in multiple formats and locations.
- Several important resources do not have a named content owner.
- Website and portal search functions are inconsistent.
- Members sometimes receive different answers depending on which staff member responds.
- Some resources are public, while others are restricted to members.
- NACPM has not established an accuracy baseline.

### Current Workarounds

- Staff search across the website, portal, SharePoint, shared drives, email, and old PDFs.
- Staff ask colleagues who may remember where the current answer lives.
- Staff copy from previously sent answers or older documents when pressed for time.
- Some staff draft responses with public AI tools, but without shared practices or approved source controls.

### Root-Cause Hypotheses

- Hypothesis: NACPM lacks a reliable inventory of common member questions and authoritative answers.
- Hypothesis: Content ownership is unclear across membership, programs, communications, and technology.
- Hypothesis: Duplicate and outdated content causes inconsistent responses.
- Hypothesis: Existing website or portal search may be underconfigured or poorly organized.
- Hypothesis: Staff are compensating for a content-governance problem with individual memory and manual search.

### Evidence Still Needed

- The top 25 repeated member questions.
- The authoritative source for each answer.
- Current content owner for each answer, if any.
- Which sources are outdated, duplicated, missing, or member-restricted.
- Baseline answer accuracy.
- Baseline staff response time.
- Whether existing search or portal configuration can resolve a meaningful share of the problem.
- Whether members prefer self-service or staff support for different questions.

### Consequences if the Problem Continues

- Continued staff time spent on repeated searches and verification.
- Inconsistent member answers.
- Risk of outdated or member-restricted information being shared incorrectly.
- Lower trust in member resources and staff responses.
- Premature AI deployment could amplify inaccurate or restricted content.

Do not treat a root-cause hypothesis as an established fact.

### Why AI Might Add Value

- Staff and members need to find current answers quickly.

### Why AI Might Be Premature or Inappropriate

- NACPM does not yet know the top recurring questions.
- Authoritative sources and content owners are not consistently defined.
- Some content is outdated, duplicated, missing, or restricted to members.
- No baseline exists for answer accuracy or response time.
- Existing search, portal configuration, or traditional automation may resolve a meaningful share of the problem without AI.

Success measures can be defined, although baselines are still needed. The workflow appears low to moderate consequence, but current access, source, accuracy, and maintenance controls are not yet sufficient for an AI-supported test.
