# Decision Model

Status: Framework v0.1 reasoning model. This document describes the reasoning model DSL uses to move from an organizational concern to a responsible recommendation about whether and how AI may help.

This is not a fixed eleven-step user journey, product screen flow, maturity model, scoring system, rubric, or assessment instrument. It is the current reasoning model for organizational change decisions where AI may or may not be relevant.

## Purpose

The decision model explains how DSL reasons from a real organizational concern toward a responsible recommendation.

The framework should begin with the organization's actual concern, not an assumed AI use case. The relevant objective may be organizational, departmental, programmatic, or workflow-specific. AI is one possible intervention, not the default.

This document complements the product charter. The charter records governing product decisions and hypotheses; this document describes the reasoning structure used across consulting, training, workshops, Decision Conversation materials, hosted application delivery, and other formats.

The central question is: What organizational outcome are you trying to improve, and where can AI responsibly help, if at all?

## Three Models To Keep Distinct

The framework needs to distinguish three related but different models:

- Conversation model: how different users enter the process.
- Reasoning model: the common decision domains the framework must address.
- Delivery model: how the methodology may be delivered through consulting, workshops, training, guided software, an AI-assisted interface, or other formats.

The reasoning model should provide a common structure across delivery formats while remaining open to revision based on evidence.

## Conversation Model: Entry Points

Different users may enter the process from different starting points. Entry points are not steps; they are the practical concerns that bring an organization into the conversation.

Example entry points include:

- An operational problem.
- An underused AI investment.
- A governance concern.
- A workflow redesign effort.
- A leadership request.
- Another discovered organizational concern.

Each entry point may carry different language, authority, urgency, evidence, and stakeholder expectations. The conversation model should help translate the starting concern into a clearer decision context without prematurely assuming that AI is the answer.

## Reasoning Model: Decision Domains

The current reasoning model includes eleven decision domains. Domains may be entered, revisited, combined, or sequenced differently depending on context. The final recommendation should address every domain that is material to the decision.

### 1. Starting Situation

Clarifies what brought the organization into the process and what is currently known. This may include an operational pain, a programmatic objective, a governance concern, an underused technology investment, an existing AI initiative, or a leadership request. This domain establishes the context, urgency, available resources, and source of the request.

### 2. Desired Change

Clarifies what the organization wants to be different. This domain helps distinguish broad aspirations from specific organizational, departmental, programmatic, or workflow-level objectives.

### 3. Ownership And Stakeholders

Clarifies who owns the work, who is affected by it, who must support it, and who has authority to make or sustain changes. This domain helps determine whether the decision has enough sponsorship and participation to move responsibly.

### 4. Current Workflow

Clarifies how the relevant work currently happens. This domain helps ground the decision in actual roles, handoffs, tools, information flows, constraints, and points of friction.

### 5. Root Problem

Clarifies what problem is actually driving the concern. This domain helps avoid mistaking symptoms, tool preferences, or leadership pressure for the underlying issue.

### 6. Candidate Interventions

Identifies possible ways to address the root problem. This domain keeps AI in comparison with other interventions, including process changes, policy changes, training, staffing, governance, data improvements, or non-AI technology.

### 7. AI Suitability

Assesses whether AI is appropriate for the workflow and problem. This domain considers fit, risk, evidence needs, data conditions, user readiness, human oversight, and whether AI would improve or weaken the work.

### 8. Prioritization

Helps choose among viable workflows or interventions. This domain considers impact, speed to value, effort, risk, readiness, strategic importance, learning value, and reusability.

### 9. Adoption Design

Clarifies what would be needed for people to use the recommended intervention responsibly. This domain includes ownership, training, governance, communications, support, evidence collection, and change management.

### 10. Bounded Implementation Cycle

Defines a limited cycle for testing and learning from the recommended intervention. This domain supports focused implementation without treating early results as proof of broad organizational maturity.

### 11. Institutionalization

Clarifies how learning, ownership, evidence, governance, and workflow changes will persist beyond the initial effort. This domain helps prevent useful experimentation from disappearing when individuals move on or attention shifts.

## Recommendation Outcomes

The reasoning model supports four responsible recommendation outcomes:

- Proceed with an AI-supported workflow.
- Prepare first, then reconsider AI.
- Pursue a non-AI intervention.
- Do not pursue the proposed intervention.

The final outcome refers to the proposed intervention, not necessarily the underlying organizational objective or problem.

Recommendations should reflect problem fit, workflow readiness, ownership, stakeholder impact, risk, evidence needs, and adoption capacity. A recommendation not to proceed with a proposed intervention can be responsible when the intervention is unclear, misaligned, harmful, unsupported, or premature.

## Prioritization Considerations

When multiple workflows or interventions are viable, prioritization should consider:

- Impact.
- Speed to value.
- Effort.
- Risk.
- Readiness.
- Strategic importance.
- Learning value.
- Reusability.

These considerations remain qualitative in Version 0.1. They are not converted into scores, weights, rankings, rubrics, maturity stages, or assessment questions.

## Delivery Model

DSL is a consulting business. The methodology may be delivered through:

- Consulting engagements.
- Workshops.
- Training.
- A hosted application.
- An AI-assisted interface.
- Other formats.

The hosted application should make the methodology persistent by recording decisions, generating working artifacts, supporting implementation, and preserving institutional memory. Delivery formats should express the reasoning model consistently. Proposed changes to the model should be documented and supported by evidence from interviews, prototype testing, hosted application use, or consulting use.

## Provisional Assumptions

The following assumptions should remain explicit and open to revision:

- The eleven domains are the right minimum reasoning structure.
- Different entry points can share a common reasoning model.
- The model can support both consulting delivery and a hosted application.
- Prioritization can remain qualitative in Version 0.1.
- The hosted application can preserve institutional memory better than static documents alone.
- The same reasoning model can support recommendations to use AI, prepare first, use non-AI interventions, or stop a proposed intervention.

## Validation Needs

The model has been exercised through a complete synthetic dry run using the Decision Conversation protocol, facilitator worksheet, decision brief template, and synthetic example materials. This supports internal coherence of the v0.1 methodology, but it does not prove field validity.

Next validation work should include:

- Testing through a runnable hosted prototype.
- Use with nonprofit and association leaders.
- Observation of where users enter the process.
- Testing whether the eleven domains are complete, understandable, and addressed in an order that fits different users, organizations, and engagements.
- Testing whether recommendation outcomes feel credible and useful.
